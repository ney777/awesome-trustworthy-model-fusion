# Model-fusion scope correction

A federated setting is not sufficient for inclusion in this model-fusion collection. The corrected catalogue requires a direct contribution to how models or modules are aligned, combined, distilled, or constrained during fusion. General federated optimization, client scheduling, gradient defenses, data sharing, and fairness objectives do not qualify solely because the protocol contains an aggregation step.

This corrects the over-broad admission at [commit `e166a13`](https://github.com/ney777/awesome-trustworthy-model-fusion/commit/e166a13feb2b4a4769597e1f454a0fa3d8487075): **17 records have been removed from the included catalogue, reducing 137 to 120**. Fifteen fall outside the corrected method scope; two lack sufficient direct-fusion evidence. The [focused method table](federated-literature.md) now contains **42 records**, each with an explicit `fusion_relevance` field. Exclusion records preserve the decisions without counting as included literature or entering README, CSV, or BibTeX exports.

## Admission rule

The inclusion test concerns the paper's research contribution, not whether its equations contain a weighted sum. A model-fusion record identifies its source models or modules, the combination or transfer operator, and the resulting model or fused capability. Direct studies of fusion compatibility, mergeability, or threats to a specified fusion mechanism also qualify. Fusion may require further training; this correction does not restrict the collection to training-free checkpoint averaging.

| Evidence supporting inclusion | Insufficient by itself |
|---|---|
| Neuron, channel or parameter alignment that enables model combination | Heterogeneous devices or subnetworks of one shared training model |
| Explicit composition of source models, task vectors, adapters or blocks | Ordinary coordinate averaging inside a generic federated optimizer |
| Multi-model teacher knowledge distilled into a student or recipient model | Sharing transformed examples, task gradients or prototype statistics without an established model-fusion mechanism |
| A fusion objective that learns model or module combination coefficients | Generic client participation, contribution accounting or update-defense weights |
| A privacy, fairness or robustness mechanism acting on a specified fusion process | A general FL privacy, fairness or robustness result |

The corrected boundary still includes federated model fusion. Model Trip and PrivFusion remain directly relevant, with their respective reading limits unchanged. FedDF, FedFTG and DENSE qualify through explicit multi-model knowledge transfer; PFNM, FedMA and GAMF qualify through matching before model combination. Their federated setting supplies context, not the admission reason.

## Retained boundary cases

The following methods remain because the inspected mechanism directly designs model fusion. They are not retained merely because they improve federated accuracy, privacy or personalization. Their local-training and data requirements remain explicit in the detailed records.

| Method | Direct fusion contribution | Source |
|---|---|---|
| FedLAW | Optimizes a global shrinkage factor and relative coefficients of a weighted combination of client-model parameters using proxy data. | [Final PMLR PDF pp.3–4, Sections 3–4.2: shrinkage and coherence; p.8, Section 6.1 and Algorithm 1: proxy-data optimization and FedLAW](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v202/li23s/li23s.pdf) |
| FedAWA | Represents local models as client vectors relative to the global model and optimizes model-wise or layerwise fusion coefficients through vector alignment. | [Author preprint v1, pp.4–5 client vectors, aggregation-weight objective and layer-wise extension](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.15842v1) |
| FedAtt | Computes layerwise attention from server/client parameter distances and uses it to combine the selected client models into the server model. | [Author arXiv v2 PDF pp.3–5, Sections III.A–D, Equations 2–6 and Algorithms 1–3: attentive aggregation and Gaussian perturbation; Section IV.A: IID random-shard evaluation](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1812.07108v2) |
| LIVAR | Merges LoRA updates with proxy-calibrated layer coefficients and combines classifier components with class-variance-aware weights. | [Author preprint v2, pp.4–6 method and proxy calibration; p.10 §6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.06813v2) |
| DapperFL | Blends local and global model parameters before capacity-aware channel pruning to construct a compact personalized model. | [Final proceedings PDF pp.3–6, Sections 3.1–3.4 and Algorithm 1: fusion schedule, pruning, domain regularization and masked-model restoration](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/17a1a1439421f1837e10cd612bf92861-Paper-Conference.pdf) |

This distinction also explains why lo-fi and the task-arithmetic/one-shot-FL analysis remain: their central questions concern terminal combination of separately fine-tuned models and the conditions of a model-merging identity. Retaining them does not require admitting every optimizer whose update can be written as an average. Likewise, learning model-fusion coefficients is different from using client-contribution scores primarily to regulate participation or general training defenses.

## History and current counts

The earlier 107-to-137 expansion is preserved as a historical decision, not rewritten as if this narrower rule had already been applied. Of its 30 additions, **23 remain and seven have been removed**. Ten older entries were also removed. The focused view changes from 63 to 42 through those 17 exclusions plus the four category moves above; those moves do not reduce the master count.

The [corrected federated crosswalk](federated-coverage-audit.md) still accounts for all 41 source occurrences and 39 identities that were screened. It now identifies **31 retained and eight excluded identities**. A source list is a discovery aid, not a rule requiring that every listed work enter this catalogue. Bibliographic verification, CCF rank, and reading depth remain separate from relevance, and none can substitute for a direct fusion contribution.
