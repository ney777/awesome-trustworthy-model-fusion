# Related surveys

These 12 surveys offer complementary routes into model fusion, trustworthy composition and related model collaboration. The table distinguishes their scope and the evidence used for comparison. It is a selected reading guide, not an exhaustive systematic review. Complete bibliographic records, source links and reading-depth details are available in the [structured bibliography](../data/papers.json).

## Choosing a reading path

For fusion mechanisms, start with *Deep Model Fusion* and the model-merging surveys, then use *From Parameters to Behaviors* to distinguish the signals transferred into a target model. For federated fusion, the aggregation, federated X and one-shot surveys help compare communication assumptions and fusion operations; the trustworthy FL survey supplies broader threat and evaluation context. Each method still needs a direct connection to model fusion before inclusion in the [federated method table](federated-literature.md).

For collaboration that retains multiple source models during inference, *Model MoErging* and the small/large-model and edge/cloud surveys provide complementary perspectives. Cai et al. exclude inference-time ensembles and source-model routing from their operational fusion definition (§2, pp.15179–15180). This distinction helps readers separate a fused target from a system that continues to select or combine source models; it does not justify including arbitrary multi-agent, RAG or MoE work. [Final paper](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-emnlp.722.pdf)

## Trust and change across these surveys

Governance and lifecycle questions are already present in this literature. Yadav et al. discuss malicious contributors, expert addition/removal and continual development (§7, pp.23–24). Yang et al. discuss trustworthy merging from owner and combiner perspectives (§6, p.29 of the author version). Song and Zheng discuss failure diagnostics, dynamic merging and safety-preserving directions (§§7.4–7.5). These are substantive connections, so the catalogue does not treat trust or model evolution as absent from earlier surveys. [MoErging author PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.07057), [model-merging author PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.07666), [FUSE preprint](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.09938)

The catalogue's [Representation → Governance → Evolution framework](taxonomy.md) is a navigation and comparison aid: what is known about source models, which fusion operations and trust claims that evidence supports, and what needs checking after a change. Privacy guarantees, measured fairness, attack resistance and deployment cost remain distinct properties with their own assumptions. Future-work proposals in a survey are not treated as implemented solutions or experimental evidence.
