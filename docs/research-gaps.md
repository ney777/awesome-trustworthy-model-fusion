# Research questions and evidence gaps

The survey follows a fusion operation from compatibility and knowledge transfer, through the trust conditions of its constructed output, to retention and reassessment after an update. The four questions below match its research agenda. They distinguish findings in the reviewed literature, comparative inferences and proposed tests. They do not claim that the corpus is exhaustive or that no other work addresses these problems. [Prior surveys](prior-surveys.md) document substantial overlapping coverage.

## 1. Predicting the value of a combination

**Evidence.** LogME estimates individual transferability; Git Re-Basin recovers parameter correspondence; Model Kinship uses task-vector relationships to select merging contributors. Their prediction targets differ. A source with useful standalone behavior can still be redundant with existing contributors or harmful under a particular fusion operator. [LogME, §3](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v139/you21b.html), [Git Re-Basin, §§3–4](https://openreview.net/forum?id=CQsmMYmlP5T), [Model Kinship](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-emnlp.32/)

**Question.** Which evidence predicts the incremental value of a source, conditional on the integration rule, other contributors and permitted information? Individual ranking supports source selection; it does not establish the value or safety of the resulting combination.

**Proposed test.** Fix the fusion operator and information budget, then predict gains and harmful additions for held-out contributor combinations. Compare individual ranking and pairwise similarity, charging any validation oracle for its queries. Separate unseen tasks, model families and near-duplicate checkpoints. A useful result must predict combination outcomes beyond correlation with standalone accuracy.

## 2. Combining trust conditions in a fusion operation

**Evidence.** FedAUX protects scoring used in teacher-ensemble distillation, while transmitting client models remains a separate channel. SafeMERGE and LED-Merging instead alter combinations using safety-related information. Their protected objects, preparation and access requirements differ. [FedAUX, method and privacy analysis](https://doi.org/10.1109/tnnls.2021.3129371), [SafeMERGE, §2 and Limitations](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-acl.1761/), [LED-Merging, method and §6](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.1055/)

Privacy accounting also depends on source provenance. Differentially Private Model Merging considers known mechanisms on a shared dataset; the geometry-aware private-merging preprint uses specified disjoint private-task records and a public reference. These premises cannot be interchanged merely because both procedures produce merged weights. [Differentially Private Model Merging](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2026/poster/149241), [Geometry-aware private merging, preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26655)

Fairness requires a separate unit and reference: FairMerging studies classwise excess loss, whereas social-bias merging experiments use particular bias and utility benchmarks. Model Trip's final-publication abstract supports cross-federation healthcare fusion with utility, privacy and fairness objectives; its detailed fairness definition, privacy mechanism and deployment protocol remain unverified. PrivFusion's mechanism evidence comes from its earlier author version, arXiv:2311.03396v1, whose perturbed matching features and weight releases require explicit protection units and accounting before supporting an end-to-end patient-level claim. [FairMerging](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v306/liu26dl.html), [Social-bias merging experiments](https://aclanthology.org/2025.paclic-1.24/), [Model Trip, publication abstract](https://researchportal.hkust.edu.hk/en/publications/model-trip-enhancing-privacy-and-fairness-in-model-fusion-across--2/), [PrivFusion, reviewed author version, §§III–V](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.03396v1)

**Question.** Under one specified fusion interface, can the assumptions behind a privacy mechanism, an empirical defense and a defined fairness objective hold together? Component checks alone are insufficient: MergeBackdoor and DAM examine attacks or defenses at composition level, within their stated threat models. [MergeBackdoor](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity25/presentation/wang-lijin), [DAM, §§2–4](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/36f7c3d80c1ba329ab8cf7b96e63a0fa-Abstract-Conference.html)

**Proposed test.** Fix the operator, trace messages and protected records, and introduce one specified poisoning threat alongside a privacy mechanism. Separately hold sources fixed while varying fusion coefficients and subgroup prevalence to test whether a fairness gain survives changes to its reference. The result may identify a feasible operating region or conflicting premises. Empirical attack resistance does not establish a theorem about composed guarantees, and an average of unrelated trust scores would conceal the question.

## 3. Retaining knowledge under restricted update evidence

**Evidence.** OPCM, DOP and NUFILT use parameter geometry to limit interference without task data at merging; ODE-M observes calibration-loss gradients. TIME shows why initialization and deployment choices belong in a temporal comparison. [OPCM, §3](https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/7611a3cb5d733e628081431445cb01fd-Abstract-Conference.html), [DOP](https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/37d9f19150fce07bced2a81fc87d47a6-Abstract-Conference.html), [NUFILT](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/hash/d0da30e312b75a3fffd9e9191f8bc1b0-Abstract-Conference.html), [ODE-M](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v306/lin26o.html), [TIME, §§3–4](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2025/html/Dziadzio_How_to_Merge_Your_Multimodal_Models_Over_Time_CVPR_2025_paper.html)

Federated continual fusion also controls how source components are produced. LoRM coordinates low-rank factor training and merging. FedSEAM restricts merged expert directions using cross-client subspace and sign agreement; its preprint evaluates near-IID client shards, with non-IID clients, secure aggregation and differential privacy left for future work. Neither result establishes retention under arbitrary source preparation or participant change. [LoRM, §3](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/d79b8c42f61b51722992705022134ef3-Abstract-Conference.html), [FedSEAM, §§3–5 and Limitations](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.33496v1)

**Question.** Which evidence detects or prevents loss of earlier behavior when earlier sources, examples or labels cannot all be retained? In federated settings, does the fusion rule remain applicable when participants cannot follow its preparation or communication protocol?

**Proposed test.** Vary source order, similarity and retained-state budget with feedback access matched within each comparison. Track per-task trajectories to separate temporary loss, recovery and persistent deterioration. Reduce old-task coverage for calibration-guided methods; test weight-only methods where geometry poorly predicts behavioral interference. Comparing different access regimes can expose a trade-off, but cannot isolate operator quality from the additional information supplied.

**Boundary comparison.** A fixed router's changing query choices do not update fused knowledge. RouteLLM and GraphRouter provide related selection comparisons. MetaRouter uses initial pairwise feedback to adapt a preference context under stationary preferences; its meta-trained encoder stays fixed at inference and its objective is one-step. This is not evidence of continuous online policy learning or recovery from changing preferences. [RouteLLM, §3.1 and §5](https://openreview.net/forum?id=8sSqNntaMr), [GraphRouter, §3 and §6](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/41b6674c28a9b93ec8d22a53ca25bc3b-Abstract-Conference.html), [MetaRouter, §§3–4 and §6](https://ijcai.org/proceedings/2026/673)

## 4. Tracing and removing contributor influence

**Evidence.** MergePrint tests whether source-linked fingerprints survive selected merges; RouteMark attributes experts through a specified routing interface. Dual-stage weight protection impairs unauthorized merging while preserving standalone behavior. Ownership signals, utility contribution and learned influence are different objects, and these results do not establish how to remove a contributor's learned effect from a consolidated target. [MergePrint](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.342/), [RouteMark, preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.01784), [Dual-stage weight protection](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2026/html/Chen_Defending_Unauthorized_Model_Merging_via_Dual-Stage_Weight_Protection_CVPR_2026_paper.html)

**Question.** What evidence distinguishes a detectable contributor signal from its retained behavioral influence, and what would establish removal relative to a defined reference? Deleting a source checkpoint or stopping calls to its endpoint does not undo earlier merging or distillation.

**Proposed test.** Record parent versions and fusion operations, then compare source deletion, reconstruction without the contributor and any proposed influence-removal method. Attribution tests need innocent models, shared ancestors and varied contribution strengths. Removal tests must assess targeted influence together with unrelated capabilities that should remain. When reconstruction is infeasible, state the behavioral tests and the resulting limits on a removal claim.

## Common evaluation requirements

These are proposed studies, not executed experiments or a new benchmark. Section VI supplies condition-matched reporting and an event-indexed protocol for specified source arrivals, replacements, shifts, compromise and withdrawal. Existing resources provide starting points: FusionBench separates pools, algorithms and tasks; KnOTS exposes the importance of task-specific versus shared label-space evaluation. RouterBench supports only the related cost–quality selection comparison, within its recorded-output setting. [FusionBench, §2](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/v26/25-1243.html), [KnOTS, §5.3](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/0d4f8a5109c5083b5307fcd0bddae7a7-Abstract-Conference.html), [RouterBench, §§3 and 6](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.12031)

The comparisons should identify when a fusion recipe is useful, when its premises fail and when an update needs further evidence. Generic federated optimization or governance questions are outside this agenda unless they change the fusion operation or its assessed consequences.
